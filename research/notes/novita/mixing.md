# mixing — Novita Listyani / Seraphine Lishe

Paraphrased from the creators' videos (all rights reserved; facts only). Every bullet links to a claim in `data/claims-novita.jsonl` (id, video, timestamp, evidence label). Dates are video publication dates — her methods changed over time.

## Synthesis

- **Order (2023–26):** liquids and the broken-up tangzhong go first, then poolish, flour, salt and sugar. Yeast is whisked into dry flour, never proofed in water. Butter goes in only after 3–5 min of gluten development, then mix 5–8 min more to windowpane. Use bassinage (hold back 1/4 of the liquid) for wet doughs, split 50:50 or 40:40:20.
- **Windowpane** is a home alveograph (biaxial stretch). A dough that fails it straight after mixing can pass after rests, folds or shaping. No-knead doughs should be tested after the final fold.
- **Autolyse** (Calvel): 3–5 min mix, 15–30 min rest, no more than 60 min. Its benefits come from resting and are not unique to autolyse. Salt is held back to allow some proteolysis, not to protect the yeast.
- **Folds:** for high hydration, 4–8 rounds of stretch-and-fold, 20–60 min apart (30 min in the 2026 ciabatta). Slap-and-fold suits wet doughs only. Rests of 30–60 min rebuild glutenin macropolymer.

### mixing.autolyse

- Calvel formalized autolyse in 1974 to counter mass-production quality loss: it shortens intensive mixing, which reduces lipoxygenase-catalysed oxidation (off-aromas, bleached carotenoids), and gives more extensible dough, easier shaping, better volume, finer and softer crumb; he noted particular benefit for sourdough. *[literature, high; novita-0103; [8ICSP9xq9o0 1:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ICSP9xq9o0&t=68s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.1007/978-1-4757-6809-1]*
- Calvel's autolyse is mixing flour and water slowly for 3–5 min then resting 15–30 min (rarely over 30); in that window little amylase or protease activity occurs, so claims of large flavour gains from autolyse are weak compared with poolish/preferments or tangzhong. *[literature, high; novita-0105; [8ICSP9xq9o0 9:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ICSP9xq9o0&t=596s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.1007/978-1-4757-6809-1]*
- Rest beyond about 60 min reduced glutenin macropolymer (GMP) content in a medium-strength flour study (protease/microbial disulfide cleavage), so ~60 min is a practical upper limit for autolyse. *[literature, medium; novita-0106; [8ICSP9xq9o0 11:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ICSP9xq9o0&t=702s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.3390/molecules26030541]*
- The benefits of autolyse come from resting and occur wherever the rest is placed (at the start, between folds, during bulk); if you skip autolyse, add extra rest or another set of stretch and folds. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0108; [8ICSP9xq9o0 15:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ICSP9xq9o0&t=953s); 2025-04]*
- The real reason to delay salt is proteolysis control: withholding salt lets flour proteases soften gluten slightly (more extensible, more flavour); adding salt brakes proteolysis and tightens the dough. Calvel's autolyse omits salt simply because it was unnecessary for its goal (shorter mixing, less lipid oxidation), not to protect yeast. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0053; [doZTKqN8Lls 6:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doZTKqN8Lls&t=397s); 2025-10 — cites doi:10.1007/978-1-4757-6809-1]*
- Calvel's autolyse (a rest after mixing flour and water, before the rest of the mix) improved starch-gluten-water links, cut total mixing time by about 15% (less oxidation), and gave a more supple crumb with better cell structure. *[literature, medium; novita-0712; [F2nGI0vgpCw 9:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2nGI0vgpCw&t=572s); 2026-01 — cites doi:10.1007/978-1-4757-6809-1]*

### mixing.bassinage

- Adding water gradually while mixing (bassinage) changes how the gluten network develops and gives a dough that is noticeably easier to handle. *[literature, medium; novita-0095; [PEoTSDswa58 1:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEoTSDswa58&t=66s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jcs.2019.102791, doi:10.1016/j.lwt.2021.111017]*
- Gradual water addition significantly improved extensibility and swelling index of wholewheat dough regardless of total water, so it suits whole wheat and ancient grains. *[literature, high; novita-0097; [PEoTSDswa58 8:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEoTSDswa58&t=487s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.1016/j.lwt.2021.111017]*
- In refined-flour dough, adding water in 2 equal parts, or 3 parts in a 4:4:2 ratio, gave the best tensile properties (higher elasticity and tensile strength) versus adding it all at once. *[literature, high; novita-0098; [PEoTSDswa58 8:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEoTSDswa58&t=522s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jcs.2019.102791]*
- Why bassinage works: high hydration makes glutenin very mobile, forming gluten fast but making the network fragile to overmixing; mixing first at lower hydration gives more friction and alignment and less overmixing risk, and the developed network then absorbs the later water instead of it just adding stickiness. *[literature, medium; novita-0099; [PEoTSDswa58 9:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEoTSDswa58&t=591s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jcs.2019.102791]*

- Fat, including yolk fat, interferes with gluten development and lengthens mixing. Add fat later or in portions to speed up the process. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0801; [uxlYDs03HfI 3:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uxlYDs03HfI&t=215s); 2022-08]*
- Add soft butter after initial gluten development. The dough seems to fall apart at first, then comes together and becomes smooth after 4-5 min more mixing, with a clean bowl base. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0768; [jMBp7YD3ArM 6:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jMBp7YD3ArM&t=400s); 2023-06]*

### mixing.folds

- Gluten can be built with two laminations separated by 20-minute rests instead of stretch-and-folds. She states this development needs about 15% of the energy of normal gluten building. *[literature, low; novita-0751; [Du35FWaSo1U 5:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du35FWaSo1U&t=355s); 2024-10]*
- No-knead / stretch-and-fold doughs will fail the windowpane after the initial mix; test after the final set of folds, at the end of bulk or before shaping. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0049; [7bwsLYvd6go 14:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bwsLYvd6go&t=847s); 2025-11]*

### mixing.windowpane

- The windowpane test is a home version of the Alveograph (Chopin, 1920s), which standardized the French "dough veil" test: both stretch dough biaxially into a thin membrane, the same deformation gas cells impose during proof and oven spring, so the test assesses extensibility relevant to gas retention. *[literature, high; novita-0043; [7bwsLYvd6go 5:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bwsLYvd6go&t=347s); 2025-11 — cites doi:10.1007/s00397-024-01430-6, doi:10.1038/s41598-021-84959-3]*
- Many good doughs fail the windowpane right after mixing because recipes differ from the standardized lab test (hydration, ingredients); gluten development continues during bulk rest, folds and shaping (e.g. rolling and folding for shokupan or cinnamon rolls), so a dough failing after mixing can pass after folds or shaping. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0046; [7bwsLYvd6go 11:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bwsLYvd6go&t=672s); 2025-11]*
- The windowpane film depends on hydration and flour absorption: an 80% hydration dough can pass with a more delicate film than a 60% dough; compare like with like. Oil the outside of the test piece to stop sticking; stretch gently from the centre with fingertips. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0048; [7bwsLYvd6go 12:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bwsLYvd6go&t=779s); 2025-11]*
- Historical: an 18th-century French text defined well-kneaded dough as no longer sticking to the hands; the earliest windowpane-like description she found ("paper-thin sheet") was in a 1987 book. *[literature, medium; novita-0050; [7bwsLYvd6go 1:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bwsLYvd6go&t=70s); 2025-11 — cites ref:bertrand-1771]*
