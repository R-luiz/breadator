# salt — Novita Listyani / Seraphine Lishe

Paraphrased from the creators' videos (all rights reserved; facts only). Every bullet links to a claim in `data/claims-novita.jsonl` (id, video, timestamp, evidence label). Dates are video publication dates — her methods changed over time.

## Synthesis

- Her doughs use 1.7–2 % salt on flour (pizza 2–3 %, steamed bread 0.25–0.5 %). 1.7 % of flour ≈ 1 % of dough weight.
- Salt at bread levels does not kill yeast or a starter.
- **Sourdough:** salt shifts LAB:yeast from about 20:1 to about 1:1. 2 % salt in the levain (Monheim) mellows sourness; about 0.7 % of the levain slightly increases it. LAB tolerate more salt in the cold (1.6 % optimum at 15 °C).
- **Salt reduction:** 25 % less is undetectable if cut in 5 % steps. Without sourdough, cutting salt shortens shelf life (5–6 days → 3–4 days in one study).

### salt

- Salt about 2% of the flour weight tightens the gluten structure and adds flavour. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0200; [wl7zl__jSGI 2:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wl7zl__jSGI&t=175s); 2020-09]*
- Salt is limited to about 0.25–0.5% of flour in steamed bread; it tightens gluten and can enhance colour and handling. *[literature, medium; novita-0409; [1Wwvxu8C8pk 0:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Wwvxu8C8pk&t=34s); 2021-05]*
- Salt controls yeast fermentation, tightens gluten strands, and enhances flavour and crust colour. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0459; [l78zValtZ5w 4:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l78zValtZ5w&t=272s); 2021-07]*
- Too little salt makes the dough overproof quickly, while too much stunts fermentation and gives a dense dough; she uses about 1.7% of flour. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0332; [j187SM6HwVo 4:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j187SM6HwVo&t=250s); 2022-08]*
- Salt is added after the autolyse, dissolved in water taken from the final dough water, at about 2% of total flour. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0239; [-T__DFw-awI 24:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-T__DFw-awI&t=1484); 2022-12]*
- Salt at about 1.7% of flour was chosen after adjusting a published guideline (about 1.5% at 11.31% protein) to the protein content of her flour; she notes the general health guideline of about 1% of final weight. *[literature, low; novita-0479; [qdOHmdTTs24 5:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdOHmdTTs24&t=322s); 2023-04]*
- Salt limited to about 1% of the final bread weight, following health guidelines; she sees this as a limit for taste and health. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0169; [iPaBk4U33sM 1:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPaBk4U33sM&t=66s); 2023-06]*
- In flour treated with organic acid, 1.5% salt (baker's %) raised mixing time by 8.3 min versus 0.8 min at 1.0% salt, and salt with acid greatly increased dough strength. Reducing salt by 0.5% is critical for mixing time when acids are present. *[literature, medium; novita-0725; [qpE_Hz0fJTM 19:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpE_Hz0fJTM&t=1171s); 2023-10]*
- Scaling the paper's 1.5% salt (for 11%-protein flour) to 13%-protein bread flour gives about 1.7%. She uses 1.75% salt on flour weight in her sourdough recipe. *[extrapolation, low; novita-0726; [qpE_Hz0fJTM 20:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpE_Hz0fJTM&t=1244s); 2023-10]*
- Salt is rarely used in steamed bread; when used, it stays below 0.5% of flour weight. *[literature, medium; novita-0810; [bdjvU_fpgok 14:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdjvU_fpgok&t=885s); 2023-12]*
- Salt slows yeast growth, so a dough with enough salt can take roughly double the proofing time. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0733; [f6suuWjPSF4 2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=178s); 2024-08]*
- Salt-free bread had larger air cells than salted bread, and was significantly harder after about 120 h (5 days), with no significant hardness difference at 2 to 50 h. Salt-free bread tasted more sour, acidic and yeasty. *[literature, medium; novita-0735; [f6suuWjPSF4 4:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=281s); 2024-08]*
- Longer proofing makes bread taste saltier: coarser, larger pores release sodium faster in the mouth. Pores that become too large make the crumb lumpy and slow sodium release. *[literature, medium; novita-0744; [f6suuWjPSF4 15:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=956s); 2024-08 — cites doi:10.1021/jf403304y]*
- Reducing salt may make the crumb coarser, which could increase perceived saltiness. The creator explicitly labels this as speculation. *[extrapolation, low; novita-0745; [f6suuWjPSF4 16:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=995s); 2024-08]*
- Sodium chloride strengthens gluten through ionic interactions, a weaker effect than the disulfide and hydrogen bonds. *[literature, medium; novita-0555; [xSiEKtN5whM 4:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSiEKtN5whM&t=280s); 2024-12]*
- Pizza dough salt is typically about 2.5 to 3 percent of flour weight; salt also influences lactic acid bacteria activity. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0425; [1DWHnkSwD48 12:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1DWHnkSwD48&t=779s); 2025-02]*
- Salt slows Ostwald ripening (disproportionation) of gas bubbles, giving a smaller median bubble radius. *[literature, medium; novita-0562; [dtBqv-qIFLo 5:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dtBqv-qIFLo&t=316s); 2025-05]*
- Salted sourdough is traditional: the German Monheim salt-sour process makes a (typically rye) sourdough preferment with 2% salt for a milder flavour; a 1970s US patent for sourdough French bread puts salt in the liquid starter; old-dough leavening carried salt by default. *[literature, medium; novita-0051; [doZTKqN8Lls 3:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doZTKqN8Lls&t=180s); 2025-10 — cites ref:patent-us3734743a, doi:10.1016/bs.aambs.2017.02.003]*

- Salt can be used to lower the acidity of sourdough and to control flavour, as much as or more than other tools. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0421; [DeNHqcxUJKA 0:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DeNHqcxUJKA&t=14s); 2026-05]*

### salt.reduction

- A one-quarter reduction in bread salt can be made without detection by consumers by cutting 5% steps over 6 weeks. *[literature, medium; novita-0742; [f6suuWjPSF4 14:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=849s); 2024-08 — cites doi:10.1038/sj.ejcn.1601583]*
- A 52% salt reduction in brown bread over 4 weeks of buffet breakfasts did not increase sodium from sandwich fillings. Consumption fell at a 67% reduction, but not when the bread was compensated with potassium chloride and yeast extract. *[literature, medium; novita-0743; [f6suuWjPSF4 14:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=885s); 2024-08 — cites doi:10.3945/jn.111.141366]*
- Layering doughs with different salt contents (inhomogeneous salt distribution) gave a 28% salt reduction with no loss of saltiness intensity. For bread at 1.8% salt on flour, that means below 1.3%. The effect was weaker on a trained panel and worked for about 31% of consumers in a 2019 study. *[literature, medium; novita-0746; [f6suuWjPSF4 17:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=1068s); 2024-08 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jcs.2010.06.018]*
