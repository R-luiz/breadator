# enzymes — Novita Listyani / Seraphine Lishe

Paraphrased from the creators' videos (all rights reserved; facts only). Every bullet links to a claim in `data/claims-novita.jsonl` (id, video, timestamp, evidence label). Dates are video publication dates — her methods changed over time.

## Claims (14)


### enzymes

- Enzymes active during a long rest can weaken the gluten network. *[extrapolation, low; novita-0619; [PusqiSDR090 8:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PusqiSDR090&t=492s); 2022-09]*
- Acidity from sourdough fermentation increases flour proteolysis, which softens dough; unchecked proteolysis causes collapse, so timing matters. Acidification also converts water-unextractable arabinoxylans into extractable forms, improving softness and volume. *[literature, medium; novita-0646; [hc8AiEc63bM 17:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hc8AiEc63bM&t=1043s); 2023-03 — cites doi:10.1007/978-1-4614-5425-0_8]*
- Acidic dough activates flour proteases, which can break gluten chains; if sourdough is left too long the dough can turn into a slurry. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0685; [A-feCciiAcw 3:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A-feCciiAcw&t=234s); 2023-08]*
- Maltose is produced from starch by amylase; acidity from a sour levain inhibits amylase activity, so the levain should be used at its peak. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0383; [Vpcmunba9wA 15:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vpcmunba9wA&t=919s); 2024-01]*
- Alpha-amylase is most active near pH 5.5 and is inactive below pH 4.0; sourdough pH typically 3.5 to 5, which is why many rye breads are sourdough-based. *[literature, medium; novita-0452; [jZMwJlzUvHE 10:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jZMwJlzUvHE&t=636s); 2024-02]*
- Ascorbic acid is converted to dehydroascorbic acid (DHA); if oxygen runs out in a long fermentation the dough weakens. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0623; [mz3FAr2z2pI 5:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mz3FAr2z2pI&t=350s); 2024-05]*
- Sourdough fermentation lowers amylase activity in the dough. *[literature, medium; novita-0246; [tyoILEdRiK8 5:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tyoILEdRiK8&t=358s); 2024-08]*
- Sourdough fermentation inactivates amylase in the dough. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0317; [qhvJ22U5SBI 2:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhvJ22U5SBI&t=170s); 2024-09]*
- Ascorbic acid is of little use in home baking because oxygen is depleted quickly in dough; she cites studies suggesting about 70% of oxygen is used up early. *[literature, medium; novita-0351; [UK88XjySuZA 4:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UK88XjySuZA&t=278s); 2025-03]*
- Protease treatment of the dough surface, or a protease-rich preferment brushed onto the dough, improves crispness retention; too much protease reduces crispness. *[literature, medium; novita-0442; [8fxvbLUJji8 12:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fxvbLUJji8&t=756s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.lwt.2017.10.034]*
- Brushing dough with a diluted sourdough starter or over-fermented poolish before final proof may add protease activity and crispness; she says it worked for her, with no controlled test. *[anecdote, low; novita-0443; [8fxvbLUJji8 13:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fxvbLUJji8&t=794s); 2025-06]*

### enzymes.amylase

- Why rest tangzhong cold 8–24 h (2023 reasoning): residual β-amylase keeps converting damaged/gelatinized starch to maltose; a significant maltose increase has been reported with rested tangzhong, consistent with Ke Yuwen's 24 h recommendation. *[literature, medium; novita-0117; [DjQ7EtvzK_w 14:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DjQ7EtvzK_w&t=845s); 2023-09 — cites ref:ke-yuwen-2004-thesis, doi:10.3136/nskkk.62.547]*
- α-amylase optimum pH 5.6–5.8 and 70–75 °C, inactivated at 80 °C fairly quickly; β-amylase optimum 60–65 °C, inactivated above 70 °C. More boiling water per flour → more gelatinization and protein denaturation, but also more enzyme inactivation → less maltose/sweetness. *[literature, medium; novita-0118; [DjQ7EtvzK_w 16:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DjQ7EtvzK_w&t=1000s); 2023-09]*

### enzymes.dough-conditioner

- Sorbitan monostearate can be used up to about 1% of flour as a dough conditioner. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0622; [mz3FAr2z2pI 5:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mz3FAr2z2pI&t=313s); 2024-05]*
