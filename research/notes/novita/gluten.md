# gluten — Novita Listyani / Seraphine Lishe

Paraphrased from the creators' videos (all rights reserved; facts only). Every bullet links to a claim in `data/claims-novita.jsonl` (id, video, timestamp, evidence label). Dates are video publication dates — her methods changed over time.

## Claims (40)


### gluten

- No-knead folding: four folds (one on each side) every 45 min, four times in total; folds develop gluten without degassing, so the bubbles stay for an open crumb. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0216; [7LuO4wfL-uI 6:09–7:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7LuO4wfL-uI&t=369s); 2020-04]*
- Four folding sessions 45 min apart (four folds per session, one on each side); the folds build gluten to trap CO2 for a fluffy focaccia. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0221; [0M8A_szL2kI 5:26–6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0M8A_szL2kI&t=326s); 2020-04]*
- Acid in a preferment strengthens gluten, and lower acidity brings out more flavour in the bread. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0344; [w-5B7vsHcok 3:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w-5B7vsHcok&t=188s); 2020-09]*
- Stretch-and-fold and no-knead methods develop gluten by stretching the dough, and the gas from yeast also stretches the dough during no-knead rests. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0277; [IgaR5XjdOkQ 4:42-5:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IgaR5XjdOkQ&t=282); 2020-11]*
- Glutenin gives elasticity and gliadin gives extensibility; these proteins form gluten when water is added to flour. *[literature, medium; novita-0278; [IgaR5XjdOkQ 0:44-1:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IgaR5XjdOkQ&t=44); 2020-11]*
- Lamination in early bulk: once the dough is elastic enough, stretch it thin, fold it back over itself and roll it up into layers; this builds gluten gently and exposes the dough to air. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0225; [4Cpsgir28HM 4:30–5:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Cpsgir28HM&t=270s); 2021-03]*
- Over-kneading, a very strong flour or hard water can make gluten too strong, and the steamed bun shrinks after steaming. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0776; [YR2e3Kka0TA 11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YR2e3Kka0TA&t=712s); 2021-05]*
- Repeated sheeting and folding of a steamed bread dough removes air pockets and builds smoothness. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0412; [1Wwvxu8C8pk 2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Wwvxu8C8pk&t=146s); 2021-05]*
- Kansui (alkaline salt) at about 1% of flour weight adds springiness, yellow colour and alkalinity to noodle dough. Too much adds bitterness, odour and reduces springiness. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0678; [cKSRuyBlpjQ 5:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cKSRuyBlpjQ&t=326s); 2021-10]*
- Windowpane test: stretch a small piece of dough thin enough to see light through it; the dough is ready when it lifts cleanly from the bowl and looks smooth and cohesive. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0866; [rvWSBsQLZXM 4:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rvWSBsQLZXM&t=281s); 2022-07]*
- Egg white proteins seem to make dough stronger and the crumb denser. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0802; [uxlYDs03HfI 2:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uxlYDs03HfI&t=138s); 2022-08]*
- Salt at 1.5-2% of flour strengthens the gluten network, and in her view sourdough acids combined with salt give more dough strength than either alone. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0647; [hc8AiEc63bM 11:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hc8AiEc63bM&t=672s); 2023-03]*
- Water polarity and hydrogen bonding underpin gluten network formation. *[extrapolation, low; novita-0629; [s-UiMbziZ9c 3:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s-UiMbziZ9c&t=230s); 2023-05]*
- Flour used in a tangzhong is largely denatured and contributes little dough strength; it can disturb the gluten network and reduce gas retention. Tangzhong doughs look drier and stronger than they are, so the windowpane test must be passed after mixing. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0684; [A-feCciiAcw 3:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A-feCciiAcw&t=198s); 2023-08]*
- A denatured protein network from the scald is described as resilient; she uses the windowpane test rather than dough density to judge gluten development. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0363; [FxgBEvkhnFg 20:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FxgBEvkhnFg&t=1207s); 2023-09]*
- Flour heated in a tangzhong has its proteins denatured, so that flour contributes essentially no gluten strength to the dough. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0723; [qpE_Hz0fJTM 17:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpE_Hz0fJTM&t=1029s); 2023-10]*
- An organic acid added to flour raised water absorption about 1% but roughly halved dough development time; the paper concluded that acid substantially decreased mixing time and weakened the dough. This is relevant to sourdough, which is acidic. *[literature, medium; novita-0724; [qpE_Hz0fJTM 17:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpE_Hz0fJTM&t=1067s); 2023-10]*
- A 13% protein flour is needed for this enriched roll; egg white and salt help strengthen the dough, while yolk fat and tangzhong soften it. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0552; [txzOxC86ePU 24:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txzOxC86ePU&t=1475s); 2023-11]*
- Certain lactobacilli accumulate glutathione or other thiols that reduce disulfide bonds in gluten, which lowers protease resistance and can reduce gluten strength and loaf volume. *[literature, medium; novita-0149; [bIP-8B2P1A8 37:31–38:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bIP-8B2P1A8&t=2251s); 2024-05]*
- Glutathione released from dead yeast cells can weaken gluten. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0624; [mz3FAr2z2pI 7:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mz3FAr2z2pI&t=421s); 2024-05]*
- Garlic (1% garlic powder) does not kill yeast in the cited study but weakens dough structure: a garlic compound (an alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl) reacts with gluten, reducing elasticity and raising viscosity, which lowers volume and makes bread denser and harder with more garlic. *[literature, medium; novita-0665; [VXyp8CVj3hw 1:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXyp8CVj3hw&t=71s); 2024-06]*
- Garlic and tangzhong weaken the gluten structure but can make the dough stiffer and easier to shape and divide. *[anecdote, low; novita-0671; [VXyp8CVj3hw 17:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXyp8CVj3hw&t=1046s); 2024-06]*
- Hot water partly denatures gluten, which makes the dough weaker and easier to stretch; the dough is also easy to over-mix. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0430; [hF9nH0WnY88 4:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hF9nH0WnY88&t=284s); 2024-06]*
- Salt tightens gluten by letting gluten bond to itself rather than to water, giving a stronger network that retains more gas. Dough without salt was stickier and weaker in her hand-mixed comparison, and the stickiness effect of a 50% salt cut was described as significant in a study. *[measured, medium; novita-0734; [f6suuWjPSF4 2:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f6suuWjPSF4&t=142s); 2024-08]*
- Wheat-rye blends: at least about 60% wheat flour is recommended for good volume; a 20% rye share showed no significant volume decrease. *[literature, medium; novita-0316; [qhvJ22U5SBI 1:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhvJ22U5SBI&t=103s); 2024-09]*
- Gluten network strength and stretch are shaped by the dough's microstructure; a recent colloidal-crystal study proposed a physical model for this. *[literature, low; novita-0260; [Y2lOr_OspiM 7:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y2lOr_OspiM&t=429s); 2024-11 — cites doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07453-6]*
- Gliadins and glutenins make up about 70 to 80% of wheat grain protein; gliadin about 50%, glutenin about 35%, and high-molecular-weight and aggregated proteins about 15%. *[literature, medium; novita-0553; [xSiEKtN5whM 1:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSiEKtN5whM&t=71s); 2024-12 — cites doi:10.1002/cche.10572]*
- Disulfide bonds give elasticity; hydrogen bonds are more numerous and drive dough strength. *[literature, medium; novita-0554; [xSiEKtN5whM 2:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSiEKtN5whM&t=174s); 2024-12]*
- High-molecular-weight glutenin subunits account for 45 to 70% of the variation in bread-making quality. *[literature, medium; novita-0556; [xSiEKtN5whM 7:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSiEKtN5whM&t=422s); 2024-12]*
- A higher gliadin-to-glutenin ratio gives a softer, more extensible dough; a lower ratio gives a stronger dough. *[literature, medium; novita-0557; [xSiEKtN5whM 9:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSiEKtN5whM&t=568s); 2024-12]*
- Gas bubbles in dough are stretched biaxially (like a balloon); above a gas volume fraction of about 0.7 the bubbles deform each other and the dough becomes a polyhedral foam with thin films. *[literature, medium; novita-0183; [n_bwkqy93zs 2:49–3:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_bwkqy93zs&t=169s); 2025-01 — cites doi:10.1002/cche.10699]*
- Strain hardening limits bubble disproportionation (Ostwald ripening) and coalescence: a larger bubble's film resists further stretch, so small bubbles do not lose gas to large ones, and doughs with higher strain hardening show slower bubble growth. *[literature, medium; novita-0184; [n_bwkqy93zs 4:03–5:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_bwkqy93zs&t=243s); 2025-01 — cites doi:10.1002/cche.10699, doi:10.1111/j.1745-4603.1992.tb00033.x]*
- The Alveograph W index (deformation energy) is positively correlated with bread volume, and European flours are often quoted with it. *[literature, medium; novita-0185; [n_bwkqy93zs 1:42–2:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_bwkqy93zs&t=102s); 2025-01]*
- GMP decreased by about 20% when mixing went from 3 to 5 min (mixing depolymerizes GMP); during rests beyond ~30 min water binds more tightly and depolymerized glutenin re-crosslinks via disulfide bonds, raising GMP, elasticity and lowering stickiness; rest also relaxes gluten for extensibility. *[literature, medium; novita-0107; [8ICSP9xq9o0 14:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ICSP9xq9o0&t=844s); 2025-04 — cites doi:10.3390/molecules26030541, doi:10.1016/j.lwt.2021.110920]*
- Strain hardening is needed to keep gas in the dough; it falls as temperature rises, and gluten cross-linking and starch gelatinization between about 50 and 75 degrees C matter in the bake. *[literature, medium; novita-0564; [dtBqv-qIFLo 10:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dtBqv-qIFLo&t=635s); 2025-05]*
- About 80% of flour protein is gluten protein. Low-temperature mixing strengthens and multiplies hydrogen bonds and favours stable gluten macro-aggregates; higher temperatures can irreversibly denature protein, one reason mixer overmixing (heat) harms dough. *[literature, medium; novita-0064; [FBKZzNnnRKQ 5:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FBKZzNnnRKQ&t=349s); 2025-09 — cites doi:10.1094/cchem-06-15-0123-r, doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2020.110035]*
- Calvel considered ascorbic acid (vitamin C) a harmless auxiliary that reinforces dough tenacity and tolerance and reduces over-oxidation. He rejected fava and soy flour because their lipoxygenase, combined with intensive mixing, destroys flavour and bleaches the crumb. *[literature, medium; novita-0718; [F2nGI0vgpCw 11:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2nGI0vgpCw&t=680s); 2026-01 — cites doi:10.1007/978-1-4757-6809-1]*
- RSD fermentation promotes gluten protein polymerization, giving a stronger, more complete gluten network (rheology and microscopy); starch granules tightly wrapped by gluten are expected to resist digestion, explaining the higher resistant starch. *[literature, medium; novita-0026; [GZEvf4buPf4 5:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GZEvf4buPf4&t=326s); 2026-08 — cites doi:10.1002/jsfa.12565]*

### gluten.strain-hardening

- Strain hardening: a dough's resistance to stretch increases with deformation, due to gluten network stiffening; described by van Vliet 1992 and the Considere criterion (1885). *[literature, medium; novita-0259; [Y2lOr_OspiM 4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y2lOr_OspiM&t=254s); 2024-11 — cites doi:10.1111/j.1745-4603.1992.tb00033.x]*
- Strain hardening (stress rising more than proportionally with strain) stabilizes thin dough films between gas cells against rupture and premature coalescence during proof and oven rise; it is considered a requirement for gas retention, volume and an even crumb. *[literature, high; novita-0044; [7bwsLYvd6go 7:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bwsLYvd6go&t=453s); 2025-11 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jcs.2007.08.010]*
