# starch — Novita Listyani / Seraphine Lishe

Paraphrased from the creators' videos (all rights reserved; facts only). Every bullet links to a claim in `data/claims-novita.jsonl` (id, video, timestamp, evidence label). Dates are video publication dates — her methods changed over time.

## Claims (22)


### starch

- Starch gelatinization needs at least about 60 °C in her guide (she recommends 60-65 °C for tangzhong). If the water cools below that, the starch does not gelatinize and yudane stays sticky. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0783; [a2Bhe16kvCI 4:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a2Bhe16kvCI&t=299s); 2021-10]*
- Damaged starch absorbs more water than intact starch. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0633; [uNoxI1-jA88 5:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uNoxI1-jA88&t=310s); 2022-09]*
- Adding amylose-rich cornstarch to the batter gives a crisper surface and less oil uptake. *[literature, low; novita-0265; [-ajZk1KPwO0 5:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ajZk1KPwO0&t=327s); 2023-07]*
- Gelatinized and mechanically damaged starch become substrate for amylases, which feeds fermentation and sugars in the crust. *[anecdote, low; novita-0378; [KSGaNRZArBo 10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KSGaNRZArBo&t=644s); 2023-08]*
- In a study of hard pretzels, dipping in hot water (80 °C) gelatinised almost all surface starch, while alkaline dipping at 87 °C gelatinised only about 50%; water-dipped pretzels were harder, and alkali-dipped surfaces were less gelatinised. *[literature, medium; novita-0159; [wqqDlDgVJJ0 7:16–8:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wqqDlDgVJJ0&t=436s); 2023-09]*
- Wheat starch gelatinizes around 65 °C in excess water. A 1:1 flour-to-boiling-water mix (yudane or tangzhong) exceeds the water needed. Gelatinized starch then retrogrades and can release liquid (syneresis) when refrigerated around 4 °C. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0727; [qpE_Hz0fJTM 21:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpE_Hz0fJTM&t=1280s); 2023-10]*
- Wheat starch gelatinizes at about 65 degrees C, while rice starch gelatinizes up to about 80 degrees C. *[literature, low; novita-0498; [QuhM5qSQ7T0 3:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QuhM5qSQ7T0&t=216s); 2023-11]*
- Bran binds about 1.7 to 3.2 times its weight in water, more than starch; gelatinization onset and peak temperatures rise with bran, while the completion temperature is unchanged. *[literature, low; novita-0502; [Pf5HSzBK6zo 4:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pf5HSzBK6zo&t=241s); 2024-01]*
- Sourdough bread shows only a small increase in resistant starch over yeast bread. The increase was not shown to produce measurable health effects; storage time may matter more. *[literature, medium; novita-0150; [bIP-8B2P1A8 13:38–14:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bIP-8B2P1A8&t=818s); 2024-05]*
- Full starch gelatinization in a tang mian style paste needs excess water beyond the flour's own needs. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0429; [hF9nH0WnY88 3:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hF9nH0WnY88&t=213s); 2024-06]*
- Rye arabinoxylans: water-extractable fractions improve volume and softness, while water-unextractable fractions lower volume and make bread harder; sourdough solubilises the unextractable fraction. *[literature, medium; novita-0245; [tyoILEdRiK8 6:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tyoILEdRiK8&t=396s); 2024-08 — cites doi:10.3390/foods12244475]*
- Replacing part of the tapioca starch with wheat flour (original mochi-donut blend about 60% tapioca and 40% all-purpose flour, reduced in this recipe) adds gluten, makes the dough less sticky and gives fluffier rolls. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0674; [bToyDv4PdL0 3:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bToyDv4PdL0&t=211s); 2024-09]*
- Tapioca starch clumps easily and absorbs water quickly. Mix it into the liquids as a sieved slurry before adding flour; sieving helps in humid climates where lumps form in storage. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0747; [Du35FWaSo1U 4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du35FWaSo1U&t=249s); 2024-10]*
- About 23% tapioca starch of total flour was the best level in her tests for soft rolls. Cassava flour, which carries more fibre, thickens more, so more starch is needed to match it. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0749; [Du35FWaSo1U 2:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du35FWaSo1U&t=139s); 2024-10]*
- B-type starch granules sit more evenly in the gluten network and can compact the dough; they may also promote beta-turns in the protein. *[literature, low; novita-0558; [xSiEKtN5whM 1:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xSiEKtN5whM&t=104s); 2024-12]*
- Steam in the oven gelatinizes surface starch into a thick, less permeable crust; that crust traps moisture and loses crispness faster, so steam can shorten crispness retention. *[literature, medium; novita-0566; [49JXxrvfR3Q 3:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49JXxrvfR3Q&t=216s); 2026-02 — cites doi:10.1016/j.foodres.2011.11.019]*
- Rice-flour glaze is cooked on the stove for 2–3 minutes until thick; the final rice flour to water ratio is about 1:9 to 1:10. *[creator-rule, low; novita-0575; [49JXxrvfR3Q 19:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49JXxrvfR3Q&t=1169s); 2026-02]*

### starch.gelatinization

- Cook the choux paste until a thin film forms on the bottom, or until the dough pulls away from the pan. *[creator-rule, high; novita-0604; [9OX9XNzoNXI 2:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OX9XNzoNXI&t=167s); 2020-10]*
- Excess water for gelatinization is ≥60% water content (starch:water 2:3); since bread flour is ~68% starch at 14% moisture, a 1:1 flour:water ratio is sufficient. Starch crystallites melt at ~65 °C regardless of water content between 60% and 90%, so extra water does not change the critical temperature; below 60% water, starch "melts" only at >100 °C. *[literature, high; novita-0125; [_mFYeiLzLpo 8:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_mFYeiLzLpo&t=496s); 2022-10 — cites doi:10.1002/star.19960480204, doi:10.1002/star.19800320806]*
- In a 1:2 yudane, starch granules lose birefringence (extinction cross) at ≥65 °C and gelatinization was complete at 75 °C (Yamada 2015, table 1). Wheat starch is ~25% amylose, 75% amylopectin. *[literature, high; novita-0126; [_mFYeiLzLpo 10:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_mFYeiLzLpo&t=604s); 2022-10 — cites doi:10.3136/nskkk.62.547]*
- Starch gelatinization needs excess water (>60% w/w water) and heat; a 1:1 flour:water tangzhong already meets the excess-water condition since flour is not all starch. Above ~65 °C granules swell, absorb water, burst and leach amylose; gelatinized granules can absorb up to ~20× their dry mass. The heat also denatures some flour protein, leaving less for the gluten network. *[literature, medium; novita-0111; [DjQ7EtvzK_w 5:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DjQ7EtvzK_w&t=327s); 2023-09]*
- Mechanism (2014 Japanese paper): tangzhong softens and slows staling by (1) holding moisture (more tangzhong → more retained moisture), (2) making damaged gelatinized starch accessible to flour amylases, which cut it into simpler sugars that may weaken starch–gluten cross-links and add sweetness/browning, and (3) producing amylopectin fragments with shorter outer chains that retrograde more slowly. *[literature, medium; novita-0009; [ocBV6-O5SL4 12:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ocBV6-O5SL4&t=775s); 2026-10 — cites doi:10.3136/fstr.20.1071, doi:10.3136/nskkk.62.547]*
