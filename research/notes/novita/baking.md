# baking — Novita Listyani / Seraphine Lishe

Paraphrased from the creators' videos (all rights reserved; facts only). Every bullet links to a claim in `data/claims-novita.jsonl` (id, video, timestamp, evidence label). Dates are video publication dates — her methods changed over time.

## Synthesis

- **Pan breads:** 170–190 °C. Basic Asian rolls 170 °C 15 min + 5 min bottom heat only. Shokupan 180 °C 25 min + 15 min bottom heat only; crustless shokupan 160 °C. Whole wheat loaf 190 °C 30–35 min.
- **Lean hearth:** Dutch oven preheated at 240 °C, 20 min covered at 230 °C, then 10–15 min uncovered at 200 °C. Ciabatta: 220 °C fan 10 min, then 180 °C 5–10 min. Pizza: as hot as the oven allows, or a skillet at 250 °C plus a blowtorch.
- **Steam (2025 review):** loaf volume is U-shaped against steam amount. Low (≈0.33 L/m³) or high (≥1.33 L/m³) steam gives the most volume; intermediate gives the least. Heavy steam gives a glossy, brittle crust that loses crispness faster. Hotter ovens need more steam. Covered vessels need little or no added water for wet doughs. Shio pan avoids steam altogether: a rice-flour glaze (about 1:9–1:15) gives a crust that stays crisp.
- **Chemistry:** starch gelatinizes at 55–65 °C, gluten sets at 70–80 °C, the surface stays at 100 °C while it is wet, and pita puffs only when the inside reaches 100 °C.

### baking.steam

- Without steam the dough surface dries and stiffens into a shell that limits oven rise and causes uncontrolled tearing; crust stiffening is driven mainly by drying (oil-coated dough kept expanding until ~100 °C, dehydrated dough stopped much sooner). *[literature, high; novita-0085; [1OD5Kex5v4g 3:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=183s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.tifs.2009.04.001]*
- Condensing steam caps the dough surface at 100 °C (without steam the outer layer can exceed 120 °C within 5 min at artisan temperatures), lowering the initial heating rate and prolonging oven spring; more steam correlated with more CO2 released during baking but only lowest vs highest steaming differed significantly. Err toward slightly less proof when steaming. *[literature, medium; novita-0086; [1OD5Kex5v4g 3:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=218s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2011.03.001]*
- Loaf volume vs steam is U-shaped: high or low steaming gave the largest volume and intermediate steaming the lowest (not enough condensation). Scaled to a 5 L Dutch oven, low ≈1.65 mL water, medium ≈3.3–5 mL, high ≥6.65 mL (≈0.33, 0.66–1.0, ≥1.33 L water per m³ oven); dough moisture adds to this. *[literature, medium; novita-0087; [1OD5Kex5v4g 5:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=323s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2011.03.001]*
- If you score, avoid high steam (the score closes and disappears); use low to medium steam. She speculates scoring would also recover volume under medium steam. *[extrapolation, low; novita-0088; [1OD5Kex5v4g 7:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=468s); 2025-06]*
- More steam gives a more brittle (higher failure force), glossier crust with more surface starch gelatinization (starch paste → dextrins → caramelization), a denser, thicker crust zone and a lower crust:crumb ratio. *[literature, high; novita-0089; [1OD5Kex5v4g 9:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=583s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2011.07.015, ref:lebail-2011-crispy-roll]*
- Higher oven temperature / heating rate lowers crust starch gelatinization and makes a thinner, more porous, more permeable crust (longer crispness retention); so hotter bakes need more steam for the same gloss. Heat-storing vessels (Dutch oven, stone) raise heating rate, offsetting steam's slowing effect — preheat well. *[extrapolation, medium; novita-0093; [1OD5Kex5v4g 17:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=1052s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2012.10.005]*
- Practical steam: an open home oven leaks steam (effectively low steaming even with lots of water); in a Dutch oven or under a cover a high-hydration dough may need no added water, a low-hydration dough a small ice cube or a spray. Glossiness indicates how much steam was present; too much in a small vessel gives a voluminous, glossy, earless loaf. *[creator-rule, medium; novita-0094; [1OD5Kex5v4g 21:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OD5Kex5v4g&t=1308s); 2025-06]*
- Lots of steam during baking increases starch gelatinization and makes the crust denser and less permeable, which shortens crispness retention; less steam gives a thinner, more permeable crust. *[literature, medium; novita-0441; [8fxvbLUJji8 11:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fxvbLUJji8&t=685s); 2025-06 — cites doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2011.03.001, doi:10.1016/j.jfoodeng.2011.07.015]*
