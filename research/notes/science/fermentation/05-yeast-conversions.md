# 5. Yeast forms and equivalence

The engine works in instant-dry-yeast equivalent. Other forms are converted by a factor with a range.

| form | multiply the weight by | range | dry matter |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| instant dry | 1.00 | — | about 95 % |
| active dry | 0.75 | 0.67–1.00 | about 93 % |
| fresh (compressed, cake) | 0.33 | 0.25–0.42 | 31–34 % |
| osmotolerant instant | 1.00 above 10 % sugar | 0.6–1.0 in lean dough | about 95 % |

## Sources

- **Manufacturer, professional.** Instant yeast holds about 3.3 times the solids of fresh yeast (96 %). The rule of thumb is 3 parts fresh for 1 part instant, and the best ratio depends on the fresh yeast, the recipe and how the yeast is added ([Lallemand Baking Update 2/9](http://web.archive.org/web/2003/http://www.lallemand.com:80/BakerYeastNA/eng/PDFs/LBU%20PDF%20FILES/2_9INST.PDF), "Activity", "Use Rate"). One pound of instant replaces three to four pounds of fresh ([Baking Update 1/6](http://web.archive.org/web/2003/http://www.lallemand.com:80/BakerYeastNA/eng/PDFs/LBU%20PDF%20FILES/1_6DRYYE.PDF), "Economics").
- **Manufacturers, consumer packs.** A 0.6 oz (17 g) cake of fresh yeast equals one ¼ oz (7 g) packet of dry yeast of any type, a ratio of 2.4 to 1 ([Red Star conversion chart](https://redstaryeast.com/yeast-baking-lessons/yeast-conversion-table/); [Fleischmann's FAQ](https://www.fleischmannsyeast.com/frequently-asked-questions/)). One packet raises up to 4 cups of flour.
- **Craig's chart header.** 1.000 % cake yeast = 0.42 % active dry = 0.32 % instant ([chart](http://web.archive.org/web/20151010043650/http://www.pizzamaking.com/forum/index.php?action=dlattach;topic=26831.0;attach=221784;image)). The cf-calculator page uses IDY = ADY × 0.75 and fresh = IDY × 3.
- **Dry matter.** Pressed fresh yeast 31–34 %, active dry 93 %, instant 95 % ([Technomitron, La levure de boulangerie](https://technomitron.aainb.com/constituants-pain-et-pate/la-levure-de-boulangerie/)).

The sources disagree on fresh to dry: 3–4 to 1 for professionals, 2.4 to 1 on consumer packs. The dry-matter ratio (about 3 to 1) supports the professional figure for instant yeast; the consumer figure matches active dry. The engine's range covers both.

## Handling that changes activity

- Active dry yeast recovers best when rehydrated near 40 °C. Cold water leaches cell contents, including glutathione, which slackens the dough (Baking Update 1/6).
- Instant yeast goes straight into the flour. When it replaces fresh yeast, add 2 % water for every 1 % of instant yeast (Baking Update 2/9, "Other considerations").
- Fresh yeast keeps about two weeks in the cold; instant keeps about a year unopened (Baking Update 1/6). An old block is a slow block: this is what the personal vigour factor absorbs.

## Osmotolerant yeast

A sugar-tolerant strain is slower than a regular one in lean dough (about 57 % of its activity without sugar) and faster above roughly 10 % sugar (note 4, manufacturer's figure). It is a different strain, not a different moisture. Convert its weight like any instant yeast and apply the sugar curve for its type.

## Levain is not a yeast form

A levain carries flour, water, acids and two kinds of organisms. No fixed ratio converts it to commercial yeast for both time and flavour. The engine keeps two separate models and, for hybrid doughs, adds their rises (note 4).

## Limits

- Brand, age and storage shift activity by tens of percent. No manufacturer publishes gassing power against temperature in a form that could be fitted here.
- Lesaffre's and AB Mauri's professional data sheets were not reachable; their consumer brands (Red Star, Fleischmann's) were.
