# 10. Staling, storage, reheating and mould

Machine-readable values: `model-bread-science.json`, section `staling`; claims `science-bread-0094` to `0110`.

## What staling is

Staling starts as the bread cools. Water moves from the moist crumb to the dry crust and out; the crust loses crispness and the crumb firms. Amylose retrogrades during cooling and sets the initial firmness; amylopectin recrystallises over days and squeezes out water. Amylose retrogradation is irreversible; amylopectin's can be partly undone by reheating, which gives a short-lived freshness ([IREKS, Staling](https://www.ireks-kompendium.com/en/bread-ageing-and-freshkeeping/staling)). Staling is not just drying: it happens in sealed bread too.

## Temperature

- Starch recrystallisation is fastest around 0 °C. Freezing delays it; warm storage above 55 °C at about 80 % RH would too but costs energy and invites spoilage; freezing at −18 °C is the established practice ([IREKS, Measures to delay staling](https://www.ireks-kompendium.com/en/bread-ageing-and-freshkeeping/measures-to-delay-staling)).
- For bread-like starch gels, retrogradation runs between the glass transition (about −5 °C) and the crystallite melting point (about 60 °C), fastest near the cold end ([Alpers 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods10010076), section 3.4, citing Gray & BeMiller 2003 and Slade & Levine 1987).
- Household trial over 10 days (Alpers 2021, Figure 1 and Table 2):

| storage | firmness, % of day 0 (wheat pan / rye-wheat sourdough) | moisture loss (wheat / sourdough) |
|---|---|---|
| plastic bag, fridge 8.2 °C | 424 % / 398 % | 3.1 % / 5.5 % |
| plastic bag, room 21.9 °C | 390 % / 330 % | 3.5 % / 5.7 % |
| metal bread box, 22 °C | (low) | 7.0 % / 8.9 % |
| micro-perforated paper bag, 21.3 °C | second fastest | 10.3 % / 13.5 % |

The fridge firms bread fastest; the paper bag firms it next fastest because the bread dries out.

## Keeping bread fresh longer

- IREKS: higher dough yield (more water in the crumb), sponges and sourdoughs, a hot start with falling heat and a shorter bake (more water left in the crumb), enzyme improvers that cut starch in the oven, emulsifiers, guar gum, enzyme-active malt flour.
- BAKERpedia: emulsifiers slow firming but do not make fresh bread softer; maltogenic amylases of intermediate heat stability; lipases; hydrocolloids; high sugar; long fermentations; freezing; packaging; larger, thicker products stale more slowly ([BAKERpedia, Staling](https://bakerpedia.com/processes/staling/)).
- Rye: wheat rolls lose crispness within hours; rye and mixed rye breads stay fresh for days, better as the rye share rises; coarse rye bread is given a maturing time because fresh crumb is still slightly sticky (IREKS, Staling).
- Bake degree: King Arthur's loaves baked to 88 °C stayed moister for days than loaves baked to 96 °C ([King Arthur](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2017/04/07/using-a-thermometer-with-yeast-bread); one loaf per condition).

## Reheating (extrapolation)

Reheating crumb above about 60 °C (the melting range of retrograded amylopectin) softens it again for a short time; it stales again as it cools, the amylose part does not melt back, and water already lost does not return.

## Storage for the app (extrapolation)

Room temperature in a bag or box that limits drying for a day or two; beyond that, freeze at −18 °C and reheat from frozen. The fridge slows mould but firms the crumb faster.

Storage also changes digestion: Chinese steamed bread kept 2 days at 4 °C formed more ordered starch double helices and digested slowest in vitro, compared with 25 °C or −18 °C ([Li 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods13040517)). See note 11.

## Mould

**Storage trial** (Alpers 2021, section 3.2): wheat pan bread in a plastic bag at room temperature showed visible mould from day 4–5, in a bread box (89 % RH) from day 4–6, and none in 10 days in a plastic bag at 8 °C. The rye-wheat sourdough bread (pH 4.48) moulded only in the plastic bag at room temperature, after 10 days; next to a mouldy slice it was contaminated in 4–5 days. The mould colonies from the breads produced ochratoxin A and fumonisins B1/B2, yet bread slices on day 10 held OTA below 1.25 µg/kg and fumonisins below 30.3 / 22.7 µg/kg (section 3.3). One study, two breads, three toxins.

**USDA FSIS** ([Molds on Food: Are They Dangerous?](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/molds-food-are-they-dangerous), read from the [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20260103144248/https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/molds-food-are-they-dangerous)): visible mould is only part of the organism; its root threads can run deep into a food, poisonous substances are often in and around them, and toxins may spread through the food. FSIS does distinguish foods. Hard cheese and firm, low-moisture vegetables may be used after cutting away at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) around and below the spot; hard salami may be scrubbed. "Bread and baked goods" are to be discarded because "porous foods can be contaminated below the surface"; soft cheese, yogurt and cooked grains are discarded for high moisture; for jams, microbiologists advise against scooping the mould out and using the rest.

**BfR** ([FAQ Schimmelpilze in Lebensmitteln, 11 March 2026](https://www.bfr.bund.de/assets/01_Ver%C3%B6ffentlichungen/FAQ_deutsch/schimmelpilze-in-lebensmitteln-gesundheitliche-risiken-und-wie-sie-sich-vermeiden-lassen.pdf)): mouldy food should be disposed of at once and not eaten; bread is among the most susceptible foods. The only exceptions are air-dried sausage and whole hams, where mouldy parts are cut away generously. Mould threads spread inside a food before they are visible, and almost all mycotoxins are heat- and acid-stable: cooking, frying or acid pickling does not remove them.

**Starters (extrapolation).** Neither authority mentions sourdough starters. A starter is a moist, porous flour paste, the kind of food both authorities say to discard when visibly mouldy; its acidity and the heat of baking would not remove mycotoxins (BfR). By their reasoning, a starter with visible fuzzy mould is discarded rather than rescued from an unaffected part, which contradicts the baking-book advice quoted in the articles. Grey liquid (hooch) or a dried skin is not mould and is outside this statement.

## Disagreements

- **Mouldy starter**: baking-book advice to save a starter from a mould-free part vs USDA FSIS and BfR, which advise discarding moist, porous foods with visible mould (they do not name starters; the application to starters is an extrapolation).
- **Toxins in mouldy bread**: Alpers 2021 found none above detection limits in mouldy bread slices (three toxins, one setup) vs the authorities' warning that toxins may spread through porous food. The trial is too narrow to overturn the discard advice.
