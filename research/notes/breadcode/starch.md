## Claims

### starch

- As dough gelatinizes during heating in the oven, the structure is fortified, which makes the baked bread soft and fluffy. [breadcode-0014; SF Ch 1 The history of sourdough; creator-rule]
- Reviving dried bread with a brief water bath works because the crumb starch absorbs a large amount of water. [breadcode-0774; SF Ch 11 Storing bread §11.1 Room temperature; creator-rule]
- During baking, starch absorbs water, swells and gelatinizes, which contributes to the crumb structure. [breadcode-0811; SF Glossary > Baking; creator-rule]
- A gel layer forms on the crust surface early in the bake, holding shape while the loaf stays extensible. [breadcode-2072; yt:NrKHO2xZTEU [9:18]–[9:54]; anecdote] *(low confidence)*

### starch.damaged

- Properly damaged starch improves gelatinisation and water absorption in flour. [breadcode-0272; SF Ch 5 Flour types; literature]
- More damaged starch means more surface area, so water interacts better with the flour and microbes have more substrate to start fermentation. [breadcode-0273; SF Ch 5 Flour types; creator-rule]
- Home mills often do not reach the starch damage of commercial mills; repeated short-break milling did not match commercial flour in the author's test, and home-milled dough de-gassed into flat bread in free-standing bakes, but worked well in a loaf pan. [breadcode-0274; SF Ch 5 Flour types; anecdote]

### starch.gelatinization

- In an underfermented dough, starch gelatinises first inside the largest pockets, which heat faster; once set, the pockets stop expanding and push other parts outward. [breadcode-1049; SF Ch 12 Troubleshooting §12.4 Debugging your crumb structure > Underferm; creator-rule]
