## Claims

### shaping

- Rye dough is shaped by tucking, with no folding, and is shaped in a banneton; extra flour may be added to a drier dough. [breadcode-0336; SF Ch 8 Non wheat sourdough; creator-rule]
- The wetter rye dough cannot be shaped; spread it in a greased loaf pan with a wet spatula until smooth and shiny. [breadcode-0337; SF Ch 8 Non wheat sourdough; creator-rule]
- Divide the bulk and pre-shape each piece; this restores dough strength and gives more uniform loaves. [breadcode-0351; SF Ch 7 Wheat sourdough §7.1 The process; creator-rule]
- Shaping high-hydration dough is hard because the dough is very sticky. [breadcode-0398; SF Ch 7 Wheat sourdough §7.4 Hydration; creator-rule]
- An over-pre-shaped dough may not recover; pre-shaping technique must be practised, because there is little margin for error at that stage. [breadcode-0484; SF Ch 7 Wheat sourdough §7.8 Dough strength > Pre-shaping practice; creator-rule]
- Swirls: after bulk, lay dough flat, spread filling, roll up, cut into swirls, then proof them in a container, which gives fluffier results. [breadcode-0743; SF Ch 9 Mix-ins §9.3 Techniques > Adding before shaping; creator-rule]
- Coverings: powdery or small-bit coverings are pressed onto the surface after shaping; coating a banneton or pan with seeds or oats also reduces sticking. [breadcode-0744; SF Ch 9 Mix-ins §9.3 Techniques > Covering the surface; creator-rule]
- Seed-coated buns: wet the surface or dip in water, press into seeds such as sesame, oats or flax; avoid mix-ins that char in the oven. [breadcode-0745; SF Ch 9 Mix-ins §9.3 Techniques > Covering the surface; creator-rule]
- Lay-flat method after bulk fermentation (spread filling with a spatula, roll up, cut into swirls) suits garlic/cheese rolls, garlic/herb rolls and cinnamon rolls. [breadcode-0761; SF Ch 9 Mix-ins §9.3 Techniques > Adding before shaping; creator-rule]
- Apple swirl buns: spread apples, cinnamon and brown sugar on the dough, roll together, cut into pieces with a sharp knife, dough scraper or dental floss, and place the pieces next to each other in a greased bowl to proof. [breadcode-0762; SF Ch 9 Mix-ins §9.3 Techniques > Adding before shaping (Apple swirl figu; creator-rule]
- Chop buns: chop retarded dough into pieces, dip each quickly in water, roll in a bowl of seeds, and bake directly in the preheated oven. [breadcode-0763; SF Ch 9 Mix-ins §9.3 Techniques > Covering the surface (Surface seeds fig; creator-rule]
- After preshaping, a bench rest of about 10 minutes up to an hour lets the gluten relax so the dough is easier to shape. — `{"bench_rest_min": [10, 60]}` [breadcode-0819; SF Glossary > Bench Rest; creator-rule]
- Without an extended bench rest, pizza dough is too elastic to shape. [breadcode-0820; SF Glossary > Bench Rest; creator-rule]
- Preshaping drags the divided dough pieces over the counter to make uniform dough balls, which makes shaping easier and more even. [breadcode-0869; SF Glossary > Preshaping; creator-rule]
- Scoring cuts the dough surface before baking so the loaf expands freely and does not burst unpredictably; it also gives a controlled look. [breadcode-0879; SF Glossary > Scoring; creator-rule]
- Pre-shaping (rounding) degasses the dough somewhat; skipping pre-shaping gives a more open crumb. [breadcode-1021; SF Ch 12 Troubleshooting §12.4 Debugging your crumb structure > Perfect f; creator-rule]

- Ciabatta is typically not shaped; the bulk is only cut into pieces, worked as little as possible. [breadcode-1024; SF Ch 12 Troubleshooting §12.4 Debugging your crumb structure > Perfect f; creator-rule]
