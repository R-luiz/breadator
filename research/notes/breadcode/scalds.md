## Claims

### scalds

- Brühstück is a German scald: hot or boiling water is poured over whole grain flour or crushed grains, the mix is cooled and added to the dough, which helps moisture retention and can improve flavour and texture. [breadcode-0823; SF Glossary > Brühstück; creator-rule]
- A Kochstück heats flour or grains together with the liquid, and the mixture must be stirred while heating to prevent clumping and burning. [breadcode-0849; SF Glossary > Kochstück; creator-rule]
- A scald pours boiling water over flour, grains or other ingredients and lets them cool; this gelatinizes starch, so the dough retains moisture and gives a softer crumb. [breadcode-0876; SF Glossary > Scald; creator-rule]
- A scald may extend shelf life and inactivates enzymes that are harmful to dough quality. [breadcode-0877; SF Glossary > Scald; creator-rule]
- A scald can enhance flavour and aroma and reduce bitterness in some whole grains. [breadcode-0878; SF Glossary > Scald; creator-rule]
- Tangzhong is a scald in which a small portion of flour is cooked with water or milk into a roux; it gelatinizes starch, improving softness and moisture retention, and the cooled roux is added to the rest of the dough. [breadcode-0881; SF Glossary > Tangzhong; creator-rule]
- Yudane is a Japanese scald: boiling water and bread flour are mixed 1:1 by weight (100 % water on flour), cooled to room temperature and refrigerated overnight. — `{"water_pct_of_flour": 100}` [breadcode-0886; SF Glossary > Yudane; creator-rule]
- Yudane softens bread texture, makes it fluffier, and may extend its shelf life. [breadcode-0887; SF Glossary > Yudane; creator-rule]
