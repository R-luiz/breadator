## 2. Sites and domains

- **the-bread-code.io** — recipes, tutorials, book, community, shop. Home page lists the Loafy tools and links `breadco.de/chat`, `/kofi`, `/buy-me-flour`.
- **breadco.de** — a short-link domain. The root is a joke "Secret Page Discovered" page with no content. Subpaths redirect: `breadco.de/loafy?source=yt-review` → `https://loafy.ai/loafy?via=yt-review`. Do not treat the root as an app.
- **pizza-calculator.the-bread-code.io** — the Rails app from `pizza-dough/calculator` plus the recipe page `/neapolitan-pizza-dough-recipe` (PDF link on the page).
- **loafy.ai** — the product is spelled **Loafy** (the request said "Loafi"; unrelated bakeries named Loafi exist and are not this). Free browser app.

## 3. Loafy — features and limits (page-stated, not live-tested)

Sources: [Meet Loafy](https://www.the-bread-code.io/tutorial/2026/10/09/meet-loafy.html) (10 Oct 2026), [developers](https://loafy.ai/developers), [tools JSON](https://loafy.ai/api/v1/tools), tool pages.

- **Ask Loafy**: AI answers grounded in The Sourdough Framework, names the chapter, says so when the book has no answer. 1 question/day without an account, 5/day with a free account; crumb-photo check needs an account. Disclaimers on the page: can be wrong; question text goes to an AI provider; cannot see the dough.
- **Guided journeys** with timers (bake bread: 14 steps, 24–48 h; make starter ≈ one week; stiff-starter journey), **answer library** (160+ answers by the author, English + German), **bake log** and **starter journal** (account), 38 interface languages.
- **Calculators**: bread (loaves, flour per loaf, hydration, whole-grain share, add-ins, presets free-standing / sandwich / flatbread, fast modes same-day / overnight / quick yeast), pizza (count, ball weight, hydration 55–75 %, yeast or sourdough, oven home/pizza/wood, margherita topping totals), hydration calculator, **zero-waste dough optimizer** (scale dough to containers; target weights customizable; saved containers need an account), **Bake Planner** (back-plans from the finish time; bulk time from several models, plan uses the slowest; cold-proof input 8–24 h in the API).
- **Models behind the planner/estimator** (`estimate_bulk_fermentation`, inputs: dough temp 10–30 °C, starter 1–50 % of flour, starter hydration 50–200 %, salt yes/no): Bill Wraith's 2007/08 model, "Loafy bakers" (Wraith scaled by logged bulk durations — crowd-calibrated, not controlled), and the figure from the book where it names one. The page itself says every time is theoretical.
- **Developer surface**: `GET /api/v1/tools`, `POST /api/v1/tools/<name>`; 120 calls/h per address; calculators/journeys/book contents keyless, book + answer search needs a free-account Bearer key; MCP at `https://loafy.ai/mcp` (OAuth 2.1 PKCE; guest limited to 3 questions/day for book/answer lookup; 10 kitchen tools — journal, starters, containers — only after account consent). The paid "Loafy answers inside your page" ($25 per 1,000 answers) is **not built** (early access, nothing charged). Third-party example listed: sourdoughcalc.app (true total hydration with any starter; dough temperature with ice).
- **Tool API semantics worth knowing** (schema JSON): `calculate_bread_dough.loaf_size_g` is **flour** per loaf (500 g flour ≈ 860 g dough: 500 + 300 water + 50 starter + 10 salt); default hydration 60 %, whole grain 20 %; `calculate_pizza_dough` default ball weights follow the oven (250 / 270 / 280 g); out-of-range inputs are clamped and reported in `notes`.

## 5. Version drift and disagreements (record both sides)

These need to go into `SUMMARY.md` if claims are extracted:

1. **Bulk end-point rise.** Old repo recipe: +10–20 / 20–40 / 40–70 / 70–110 % by protein tier. Website (Oct 2026): +25 / +50 / +100 / >100 %. Video: start at ≈ +25 %. Loafy planner and bread-calculator FAQ: ≈ +50 % for 10–12 % protein. Pizza repo README and recipe: ≈ +50 %. Tier boundaries overlap/leave gaps in both tables (e.g. "12–15" vs "13–14").
2. **Starter amount.** Old repo: 20 %. Website: 5–10 % ("instead of 100 g"), video: 10 % (2.5–5 % when hot, ">20 % never"). Loafy planner: 10–20 % at 20 °C, 5–10 % at 25 °C, 1–5 % at 30 °C, up to 30 % "in a very cold kitchen" — contradicts "never more than 20 %". Denominator is flour weight, not inoculated flour.
3. **Core temperature / proof / autolyse.** 95 °C (old recipe, FAQ) vs ≈92 °C (website); proof 2–4 h (old) vs 30 min–3 h (website); freezer before scoring 45 min vs 30 min; overnight autolyse (old) vs none (website).
4. **Hydration.** Old FAQ: ≈70 % ideal, ≥70 % for bubbles; website/Loafy: 60 % default for most flours ("the book recommends around 60 %"), protein-tiered 55–70 %.
5. **Salt base.** Old FAQ: 2 % of total dough weight including starter; website, calculators: 2 % of flour weight.
6. **New starter feeding.** Old repo: 1:1:1 (50/50/50) from day 2; website explicitly rejects 1:1:1 in favour of 1:5:5 (10 g + 50 + 50) from day 2.
7. **Pizza method.** README: bulk first, divide 3 h before baking, 8–12 h to +50 %. Website: ball-first, boxes, 12–14 h, whole box to the fridge. Same 60/2/0.05 % formula. Website also warns >48 h fridge is pointless, Jose Lausuch's recipe uses 24 h–3 days.
8. **Cold fermentation.** Website pizza: "at around 4 °C, after a while, nothing happens at all"; video: "almost all microbial activity stops" at 4 °C and microbes "turn into spores" after weeks (caption text, likely mistranslated and biologically doubtful); the old experiment shows more oven spring after a fridge proof. Do not turn these into mechanistic claims without checking against science sources.
9. **Dry yeast dose.** 0.05 % dry (pizza, ≈12–14 h at unspecified room temperature, below 20 °C double it) vs 0.2 % (basic bread, ≈8 h bulk) vs 0.25 % (hydration experiment, overnight fridge) vs 1 % (1–2 h pizza). Fresh:dry is consistently **3:1** (pizza, basic bread, burger buns 6 %:2 %, quick pizza 3 %:1 %).

## 6. Calculator mechanics to avoid copying wrongly

- The open-source pizza calculator solves flour from the target ball weight and treats starter as one added mass: for 4 × 270 g with 5 % starter, flour 647 g, water 388 g, starter 32 g. If the starter is 60 % hydration, true flour is ≈667 g and water ≈400 g (true hydration ≈60 %); with a 100 % starter the true hydration would be ≈ 58 %. Any Breadator implementation should split starter flour/water explicitly and show both added-ingredient % and total %.
- Bill Wraith's inoculation: for added flour F, starter S and starter hydration h (fraction), inoculated flour % = (S/(1+h)) / (F + S/(1+h)); the page's 2/5/10/15/20/30/50 % columns match this at h = 1 (e.g. 10 g per 100 g F → 4.76 % ≈ 4.8 %). The table assumes 100 % starter; a stiff starter changes the denominator, not just the label.
- Loafy bread calculator: percentages are of added flour (starter flour not netted); 860 g dough for 500 g flour is additive, not net of starter water.

### 6a. `pizza-dough/calculator` formulas (MIT, © 2023 Hendrik Kleinwaechter; commit `fce9b125`)

Source: `calculator/app/services/dough.rb` (whole file), form `calculator/app/views/static/index.html.erb` L13–L45, params `calculator/app/controllers/static_controller.rb` L17–L33. Inputs (all as fractions of flour): `hydration` *h* (form shows %, values > 1 are divided by 100), `pizzas` *n*, `weight_per_pizza` *W* (g), `dough_type` yeast|sourdough, `yeast` *y* (hidden field, fixed 0.0005), `sourdough` *s*, `salt` *k*.

| Quantity | Formula | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Flour *F* | *F* = *W* / (1 + *h* + *k* + *a*) × *n*, where *a* = *y* (yeast dough) or *s* (sourdough) | *h* 0.60, *W* 250 g, *n* 2, *k* 0.02 |
| Water | *F* × *h* | 60 % |
| Salt | *F* × *k* | 2 % |
| Dry yeast | *F* × *y* (shown to 0.01 g) | 0.05 % |
| Fresh yeast (alternative) | *F* × *y* × 3 | 0.15 % (fresh:dry = 3:1); **not** included in the flour equation, so a fresh-yeast dough is ≈0.1 % of flour heavier than *W* |
| Starter | *F* × *s*; treated as one added mass, its flour and water are not netted into *F* or *h* | 5 %, recommended stiff ≈60 % hydration |
| Toppings per pizza | linear interpolation through (250 g → 280 g ball): mozzarella 80→100 g, sauce 60→80 g, oil 6→8 g, basil 16→20 leaves; totals × *n*; basil pots = ceil(total leaves / 80) | extrapolates linearly outside 250–280 g |

Worked example (yeast, defaults): *F* = 250 / 1.6205 × 2 = 308.5 g flour, 185.1 g water, 6.2 g salt, 0.15 g dry or 0.46 g fresh yeast. Sourdough, 4 × 270 g: *F* = 270 / 1.67 × 4 = 646.7 g, water 388 g, salt 12.9 g, starter 32.3 g.

Text attached to outputs: dry-yeast note says the dose suits a room at ≈22 °C (70 °F), use slightly less when warmer and more when colder, and keep fermentation slow. Starter note recommends a 60 %-hydration stiff starter (more yeast, less bacterial activity → fluffier, less sour) and points to "section 4.3" of the book; in the current book stiff starters are §4.4 (§4.3 is liquid starter) — a stale cross-reference. Legacy query parameters (`count`, `water`, `weight`, `sourdoughPercent`, `dryYeastPercent`) map onto the new names; `sourdoughHydration` is accepted by the controller but unused by `Dough`. There are no min/max bounds in the model; the form imposes none beyond HTML number inputs.

For Breadator: reuse the closed-form flour solve, but (1) include every added ingredient (fresh yeast, oil, sugar) in the denominator, and (2) split starter into flour *s*/(1+*h*<sub>s</sub>) and water *s*·*h*<sub>s</sub>/(1+*h*<sub>s</sub>) to report true hydration and salt on total flour.
