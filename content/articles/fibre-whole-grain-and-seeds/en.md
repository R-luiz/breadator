Wholemeal flour is the whole grain ground; whiter flours have had the outer layers removed by mills and sieves [@breadcode-0249]. A wheat kernel is about 14 % bran, 83 % endosperm and 2.5 % germ [@novita-0631], and in French flours fibre roughly triples from T55 to T150, from 3.9 to 12.5 g per 100 g [@bourre2008].

## What bran and germ do to a dough

**Water.** Wholemeal flour absorbed 5.5–8 points more water on the farinograph than refined flour milled the same way, and 8–10 points more in the bake [@pradhan2026]. A common home rule adds less, about 5 g of liquid per 120 g of whole wheat flour, some 4 points [@science-flour-0039]. Bran takes up water fast, leaving less free water for the gluten at first [@breadcode-0471].

**Gluten.** Gluten makes up 80–90 % of the protein of white flour but 60–80 % in wholemeal, because the outer layers add other proteins [@geisslitz2019]. Bran also disrupts the network and lowers loaf volume [@novita-0537], and fibre reduces gas retention and tolerance [@science-ferm-0032]. Hence the advice to use a strong whole-wheat flour, about 13.5 % protein or more, for a 100 % wholemeal loaf [@novita-0032; @breadcode-0382].

**Fermentation.** Most of the grain's enzymes sit in its outer layers, which is why wholemeal reacts faster [@poudel2018]. Bakers find that whole-wheat doughs ferment faster [@breadcode-0384], and one baker's whole-wheat dough overfermented quickly [@breadcode-0041]. The falling number, an indirect measure of α-amylase [@gmbhnd], does not show it: a finely milled wholemeal wheat read 324 s against 320 s for a white flour [@wojciechowiczbudzisz2023].

**Volume.** White bread reached 4.68 cm³/g in one study; replacing 20 % of the flour with oat bran gave 4.21 cm³/g when the bran was hydrated to saturation first, and 3.53 when it was not [@wu2025].

## What bakers do about it

- **Water and a rest.** Wait longer or add a little water [@breadcode-0472]; 15 minutes of soaking hydrates the bran before kneading [@breadcode-2076]. A long autolyse is another matter: after weeks of whole-wheat trials, not a controlled comparison, The Bread Code's author skips it, because the extra enzymes make everything run faster [@breadcode-1482; @breadcode-1501].
- **Soak the bran apart.** Novita Listyani soaks bran for about 3 hours so it softens and does not cut the gluten [@novita-0469].
- **Scald it.** Boiling water poured over wholemeal flour or crushed grain helps the crumb hold moisture [@breadcode-0823]; one study found gelatinized flour with extra water made a better brown-flour bread [@parenti2019]. See [tangzhong, yudane and other scalds](/en/learn/tangzhong-yudane-scalds).
- **Sift or blend.** Sifting is optional [@novita-0537]; one suggested blend is a quarter to a half part of whole wheat per part of bread flour [@science-flour-0039].

## How much water each addition takes

Two figures should not be confused: the water an ingredient holds when soaked to saturation, and the extra water a dough needs, at the same consistency, when it goes in. Soaker values come from a German baking lexicon [@science-flour-0068].

| Ingredient | Soaker water per 100 g | Extra dough water per 100 g |
|---|---|---|
| Wheat bran | 200 g | about 100–110 g, estimated |
| Oat bran | 160 g, saturation [@wu2025] | about 160 g, taken from the saturation figure |
| Psyllium husk | 2,000–4,000 g | about 950 g, one study [@science-flour-0065] |
| Chia, ground | | about 550 g, one study [@science-flour-0065] |
| Linseed, whole or cracked | 150 or 200 g | |
| Sunflower, sesame, pumpkin seeds | 50 g | |
| Cracked grain, oat flakes, quinoa | 100 g | |

Where no dough figure exists, the flour calculator counts the soaker figure as the water the addition brings.

**Wheat bran.** Treating wholemeal as about 83 % white flour plus 17 % bran and germ [@novita-0631], and giving the whole refined-to-wholemeal gap of two mill types to that fraction [@pradhan2026], one gets about 1.0–1.1 g of water per gram of bran: arithmetic on one study, not a measurement.

**Psyllium: the sources disagree.** At equal consistency, a wholegrain wheat dough took 55 g of water per 100 g of flour, 100 g with 5 % psyllium husk in place of flour and 80 g with 5 % ground chia [@torbica2025]. Yet gluten-free studies added up to 10 % psyllium without adjusting the water, and in wheat bread about 3.5 % psyllium at 75 % hydration worked best [@strkalj2025].

**Chia and flax.** Flax mucilage, about 6 % of the seed, binds 16–30 g of water per gram. In wheat dough, 5 % chia flour left the farinograph absorption unchanged while 15 % raised it, and both seeds reduce extensibility [@science-flour-0067].

**Whole seeds and grains.** Flax, sunflower and sesame seeds draw a little water from the dough during fermentation, so The Bread Code adds 1–2 % more [@breadcode-0718]. Whole wheat or rye kernels are soaked overnight or boiled for 10 minutes [@breadcode-0737; @breadcode-0738], then give water back, so a little more flour may be needed [@breadcode-0739]. Soakers rest 4–20 hours at 10–30 °C, or 8–12 hours at 6–8 °C [@science-flour-0068]; too much soaker water shows as streaks, a sticky crumb and lower volume [@gmbhnd].

## β-glucan from oats and barley

EU law allows a cholesterol claim for food with at least 1 g of oat or barley β-glucan per portion, the effect coming with 3 g a day, and a claim on blood glucose after a meal at 4 g per 30 g of available carbohydrate [@commission2012]. For bread the second means roughly 5.9–7.3 g of β-glucan per 100 g, an estimate; flour or bran weight is not β-glucan weight [@science-bread-0122], and the flour's own β-glucanases may cut it during a long fermentation, so only an analysis of the baked bread would tell [@science-bread-0047]. In the dough, β-glucan was the fibre that most improved the prediction of water absorption in UK flours [@lovegrove2020]; with no per-gram figure for it, oat bran's 1.6 g per gram is the nearest measured value [@wu2025]. [Bread and nutrition](/en/learn/bread-and-nutrition) reviews the health studies.

## Where the tested ranges stop

The psyllium and chia figures come from a single study, at a 5 % dose [@torbica2025]. The oat-bran result holds for 20 % of the flour replaced, and the best dough was softer than the farinograph standard, 366 against 500 units [@wu2025]. Seed additions kept sensory acceptance up to 10–15 % [@science-flour-0067]; an industry handbook caps boiled grain at 15 % of the flour [@gmbhnd]. The [flour calculator](/en/calculators/flour) adds the water of bran, seeds and psyllium to a starting hydration and flags more than about 15 % of bran and seeds together, or 5 % of psyllium, as beyond the ranges its coefficients come from: start from its figures, bake and note the result. [Water absorption and hydration](/en/learn/water-absorption-and-hydration) explains the method, and the [formula calculator](/en/calculators/formula) counts a soaker's water in the total.
